# ICR 0004 (WP-04): network sidecar conventions other packages rely on

No change to `src/model/**` or `src/platform/ports.ts`. WP-04 implements `NetProxyFactory`/`NetProxy` (§2.7.7) exactly as typed. Three conventions sit on top of those types. Consumers need them:

1. **Sidecar death on the record stream (WP-08, WP-12).** `NetProxy` has no "died" event. When the control connection drops and `stop()` was not called, the client emits exactly one reserved record: `{ tunnelId: 0, kind: 'error', token: 'none', host: '', port: <proxy port>, error: 'sidecarDied' }`. The constants are exported from `platform/netproxy/client.ts` as `SIDECAR_TUNNEL_ID` (0) and `SIDECAR_DIED`. Real tunnels are numbered from 1. After that record, `bindClaudePid`/`setFault` reject with `NetSidecarError{reason:'down'}`. `stop()` still resolves and makes sure the process is gone. WP-08 should map the reserved record to `channel.health{channel:'network', health:'down', reason:'sidecarDied'}`, not to a tunnel phase. Per §3.10, L3 then kills the pane with `E_NETWORK_UNAVAILABLE{reason:'sidecarDied'}`. `NetProxy.pid` can also be polled with `ProcessPort.isAlive`.
2. **Errors (WP-11 errors-map).** The client throws `NetSidecarError` (`name === 'NetSidecarError'`, `reason ∈ configExists | spawnFailed | exited | startTimeout | handshake | requestTimeout | down | stopped | rejected`). Map it to `E_NETWORK_UNAVAILABLE`. Messages never contain tokens. On start failures they include the sidecar log tail.
3. **Construction (WP-INT composition root).** Use `createNetProxyFactory({ process, clock, logger, nodePath: process.execPath, ownerPid: process.pid })`. `scriptPath` defaults to `dist/platform/netproxy/standalone/sidecar.js`. `sidecarEnv` defaults to `{}`, because the sidecar needs no env. The sidecar honours `sidecarConfig.resolveOverrides` (a test-only routing table) only when its own env has `NODE_ENV=test`. The config file is `<dirname(controlSocketPath)>/net.json`. It is written with `O_EXCL`, mode 0600, and the sidecar deletes it after reading. Before `bindClaudePid`, the sidecar exits 5 s after the owner pid dies. After a bind, it exits 5 s after Claude's pid dies.

Record cadence (for WP-08's tunnel tracker): the first chunk of each direction after ≥ 100 ms of quiet is emitted at once, so burst starts keep their exact time for the ±250 ms join. Later chunks are coalesced into ≤ 10 records/s per direction. `at` is the time of the last chunk in the record. `records`/`lastRecordLen` count complete TLS records in that record, so a 22-byte terminator shows as `lastRecordLen: 22`. `sni`/`alpn` are on the first `up` record. `close.bytes` = up + down totals. `refused.error` is the reason: `auth`, `port`, `blockedAddress`, `privateAddress`, `tunnelCap`, `fault:refuse`, `connectTimeout`, `method:<M>`, `badHead`, `badTarget`, or a real errno such as `ENOTFOUND`/`ECONNREFUSED`.

Verifier notes (WP-04 audit): `sidecarConfig.subscriberHighWater` (default 1 MiB) sets how much one control subscriber may buffer before its events are dropped (`stats.dropped`); tunnels never wait on it. Besides SEC §6.3's list the policy also refuses `::/96` (IPv4-compatible), multicast (`224.0.0.0/4`, `ff00::/8`) and `255.255.255.255`. A client that sends FIN before finishing its CONNECT head frees its pending-head slot at once. A client that leaves while the upstream dial is still pending is paused, so the sidecar sees it go only after the 504 or the connect. Either way the tunnel gets exactly one terminal record.
